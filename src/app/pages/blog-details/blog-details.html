Before moving ahead, let’s look at a few commands that use HEAD. If you want to understand these commands, you must first understand what Git HEAD is first. 


    git checkout HEAD~1
    git reset HEAD^1 --soft
    git reset HEAD^1 --hard
    git diff HEAD
    git reflog


What is Git HEAD ?

Imagine you are writing a book, and each page you write is a new commit. In this analogy, HEAD acts as a bookmark that shows you which page (commit) you are currently on. As you flip through the pages, HEAD moves with you, pointing to the current page. You can also jump to any page to see its content.

One important difference from real life: when you read a physical book, you only see the content of the page you're on. In Git, however, when HEAD points to a commit, now being at head pointing to that commit you can see content of all previous chnages.

If HEAD is just a pointer or bookmark pointing to a particular commit, then what is a Detached HEAD? 🤔 Well, when HEAD points to a commit other than the latest one in a branch, it is said to be in a Detached HEAD state.

Analogy:
Imagine you’re reading a big book where each page = a commit. Normally, you have a bookmark (HEAD) pointing to the last page you’re working on, so when you keep writing new pages (commits), they are added after the bookmark.

Normal HEAD: Bookmark on the last page → new pages always continue the story.

Detached HEAD: Suppose you remove your bookmark and flip to page 50 just to read it. You are not moving the bookmark—you’re just looking at that page. If you start writing new pages from there, they’re not attached to the main storyline (branch). It’s like writing alternate endings in a side notebook, without disturbing the main book.

Why is this important?
Detached HEAD is super useful when experimenting with new features. You can check out any commit and branch off from there. also it get handy if you want to checkout some random commit conntent. 


    git checkout <commit-hash>
    git checkout -b new-branch

Best Practices

  • Check whether HEAD is attached or detached before committing.
  • Use git reflog to see HEAD history.
  • Create short-lived branches for experiments.
  • Double-check commands that move HEAD (git reset, git checkout).

Conclusion

In one sentence: HEAD is where you are in Git history.

“Comfort is a trap”: Software engineer’s 15-year journey ends with hard truths

Job Loss · Software Engineer Layoff 2025 — Reflections after nearly 15 years at the same company

A software engineer’s candid post on Reddit has struck a chord with professionals navigating job insecurity and career transitions. After working at the same company for nearly 15 years, the user revealed how sudden funding issues and restructuring forced him to resign, leaving him back on the job market for the first time in over a decade.

From Stability to Sudden Exit

According to the post, the engineer stayed with the firm through multiple rebrands and funding rounds, especially in the aftermath of COVID-19. Despite hopes of funding, the situation turned grim this July. “My boss was let go soon after, and by mid-August, it was my turn. They asked me to resign instead of an official layoff, and I did, at the end of August,” the user wrote.

The professional admitted that recognition for his work was rare but said he stayed on due to comfort, constant challenges, and trust from leadership. “Once it sank in, I felt the pain: being asked to leave and realizing I never truly got the recognition that could have motivated me.”

The Harsh Realities of Job Hunting

Now back on the job market after more than a decade, the engineer has already applied for 60–70 roles with little response. While a few rejections indicated his profile was reaching recruiters, the overall silence has been difficult.

“You’re always replaceable, performance isn’t enough; business matters more. Don’t stay too long in one place; comfort is a trap. Keep open communication with managers/teammates about company direction.”

Life Lessons Beyond the Job

Other advice included staying adaptable, interviewing regularly to gauge market value, and maintaining updated resumes and LinkedIn profiles. “Work hard only if you’re happy and still growing; otherwise, reconsider. Keep interviewing occasionally to know your worth and market trends. Build and maintain a network; it pays off later.”

Despite the setback, the engineer remains hopeful, crediting his partner’s support for keeping him grounded. “I turn 40 in a few days, and I’m hopeful this will be the start of a new chapter,” he added.
